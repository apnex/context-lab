# context-lab - vision

**Status: draft, being written with the director; not ratified.**

---

## North star

Any project can find out, by experiments anyone can repeat, how the context it gives its agents changes what they understand and do.

**Terms, as this vision uses them.**

- **Context** - everything a project puts in front of an agent: instructions, documents, skills, and the manner, amount and moment of their arrival.
- **What they understand and do** - both comprehension and behaviour; each is measured, and neither stands in for the other.

Chosen by the director on 2026-10-10 over three narrower and broader candidates: the effect of guidance alone, which left out delivery and timing; any claim about agent behaviour, which reached past one duty; and the effect of a change alone, which left out learning and value.

---

## What this is, and what it is not

**It is:**

1. **An instrument for experiments on context.** Context is what varies; everything else is held fixed and proven fixed - isolation, the pinned revision, the model.
2. **A shared method.** One set of rules for isolation, blinding, judging and invalid runs across projects, so results from two projects can be compared.
3. **A durable record.** Experiments, the rules they set in advance, and their results are kept so anyone can re-run and compare them.

**It is not:**

4. **A benchmark of models.** A model is a factor in an experiment, never its subject.
5. **A target to tune context against.** Scores are evidence about context, not goals; guidance rewritten to pass a scenario, rather than to help agents work, defeats the purpose.
6. **The owner of any project's scenarios, keys or conclusions.** Each project owns those; this owns only the method.
7. **Judgement by agents alone.** Where a human is the one the work is for, agent judges are checked against that human.
8. **Tied to one harness or model vendor.** A harness or model is an adapter, and adding one does not change the method.

All eight accepted by the director on 2026-10-10.

---

## Succeeding

Measured on five dimensions, never one score, and the first outranks the rest.

1. **Findings change decisions.** Projects' choices about context cite experiments run here - what is always-on, whether a rewrite helped. It has failed if experiments run and nothing is decided differently.
2. **Adoption.** Projects run experiments here rather than building their own runners, and existing copies are retired. It has failed if another project copies the runner again.
3. **Repeatability.** Any recorded experiment re-runs from its record and reproduces within the noise it stated. It has failed if a result cannot be re-run, or re-runs differently with nothing changed.
4. **Findings travel.** A finding from one project is tested in another, confirmed or refuted, and recorded either way. It has failed if every finding stays where it was made.
5. **Honest reporting.** Every result carries its limits, and an experiment changed after its outcomes were seen is marked calibration, never reported as a result. It has failed if a headline outruns its caveats.

The first outranks the rest because this is a research instrument: a component used everywhere and perfectly repeatable that changes no decision has missed its north star.\
Cost is measured, not a measure of success.

Accepted by the director on 2026-10-10.
